# ICT Trading Methodology Explained: How Smart Money Moves Markets

The Inner Circle Trader (ICT) methodology has transformed how a generation of traders understands market structure. Originally developed by Michael J. Huddleston, ICT concepts provide a framework for reading institutional order flow — understanding *why* price moves, not just *where*.

## What Makes ICT Different

Most retail trading education focuses on lagging indicators, pattern recognition, and rule-based systems. ICT methodology takes a fundamentally different approach: it models the behaviour of market makers, central banks, and institutional liquidity providers.

The core premise is simple: markets are not random. They are engineered by participants with enough capital to move price. Once you understand how and why these participants accumulate and distribute positions, price action becomes far more predictable.

## Core ICT Concepts

### Order Blocks
An order block is a consolidation zone where institutional capital was deployed. When price returns to an order block, there is a high probability of reaction because institutional participants defend their positions.

**Bullish Order Block**: The last bearish candle before a significant upward displacement  
**Bearish Order Block**: The last bullish candle before a significant downward displacement

AL-MUDIR identifies high-timeframe (H4, Daily) order blocks and executes entries on lower timeframes (M15, H1) for precision timing.

### Fair Value Gaps (FVG)
A Fair Value Gap occurs when price moves with such momentum that it leaves an imbalance — a gap between the wicks of three consecutive candles. Institutional theory holds that price must return to fill these gaps, creating high-probability entry zones.

AL-MUDIR uses FVGs as entry refinement tools, entering positions when price retraces into the gap zone during a killzone session.

### Liquidity Concepts
- **Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)**: Clusters of buy stops above equal highs, swing highs, and resistance levels
- **Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)**: Clusters of sell stops below equal lows, swing lows, and support levels
- **Liquidity Sweeps**: When price extends beyond a liquidity level to trigger stops before reversing — the institutional "stop hunt"

Understanding liquidity placement allows AL-MUDIR to anticipate where price will be drawn to, and position for the reversal after the sweep.

### Killzones
ICT identifies specific time windows with the highest probability of institutional activity:

- **Asian Killzone**: 20:00–00:00 EST — Range establishment
- **London Killzone**: 02:00–05:00 EST — First expansion
- **New York Killzone**: 07:00–10:00 EST — Major moves and reversals
- **London Close**: 10:00–12:00 EST — Profit-taking and late-session moves

AL-MUDIR's execution is concentrated during these windows, where 80% of the day's high-probability setups occur.

1. **Multi-timeframe analysis** across monthly, weekly, daily, and intraday charts
